# Regulatory and Best-Practice Principles

This reference is a baseline. For jurisdiction-specific reviews, verify current requirements from authoritative sources.

## 1. US Interagency Model Risk Management Guidance (2026)
Current US interagency guidance emphasises a risk-based approach to model risk management and covers:
- model development and use,
- validation and monitoring,
- conceptual soundness and outcomes analysis,
- governance and controls,
- third-party/vendor products.

Practical implication:
Validation scope and frequency should be proportionate to the model-risk profile, bank size/complexity, model use, and materiality rather than mechanically applying one validation cadence to every model.

Authoritative source:
OCC Bulletin 2026-13, Model Risk Management: Revised Guidance, 17 April 2026.

## 2. AUSTRAC AML/CTF framework (2026)
AUSTRAC's updated framework stresses:
- ongoing customer monitoring for unusual transactions and behaviours,
- documented systems/controls,
- risk assessment and AML/CTF policies,
- periodic independent evaluation,
- review/update after significant changes,
- alignment of monitoring to customer and ML/TF risk.

Practical implication:
A validation should test not only statistical behaviour but whether transaction/customer monitoring is capable of identifying unusual activity relevant to the bank's risk profile and whether changes are appropriately governed.

Authoritative sources:
- AUSTRAC: How to monitor your customers.
- AUSTRAC: What you must monitor for.
- AUSTRAC: Step 4 – Review and update your AML/CTF program.
- AUSTRAC: Step 5 – Conduct an independent evaluation.

## 3. Wolfsberg – Effective Monitoring for Suspicious Activity
Wolfsberg promotes an effectiveness-oriented approach that moves beyond raw transaction-monitoring volume and focuses on:
- risk-based controls,
- useful information,
- broader monitoring for suspicious activity,
- typology/risk coverage,
- transition and validation,
- balance between model risk and financial-crime risk,
- explainability,
- responsible use of supervised and unsupervised ML.

Practical implication:
Do not optimise solely for fewer alerts. Validate whether changes maintain or improve meaningful financial-crime risk coverage.

Authoritative sources:
- Wolfsberg Statement on Effective Monitoring for Suspicious Activity, Part I (2024).
- Wolfsberg Statement on Effective Monitoring for Suspicious Activity, Part II: Transitioning to Innovation (2025).
